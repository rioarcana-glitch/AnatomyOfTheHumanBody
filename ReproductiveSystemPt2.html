The Reproductive System

The reproductive system is a unique one in our project as it not only one of the smallest but also the only one that A varies between male and female, but B cannot function in harmony without two of them! We will be splitting this part of the project into two sections to help understand. This system is incredibly important to the livelihood of the human race providing a way to breed and multiply, despite its small size and low usage rate it is key to the survival of us and how we survive. So how does it work?

As a light break down and overview of how the systems work, we will explain: in minor details the sperm cells travel from the male organs into the females where they meet the egg cells and just one out of the billions that make the journey fertilises it. It then slowly grows into a child. Now we will dive into more detail.

The Female Reproductive System

The female reproductive system can be broken down into the vagina, the uterus, the cervix, the ovaries, the fallopian tube and the endometrium.

The vagina acts as a channel, as anything that comes in or out of the female reproductive system passes through it. It works to protects the rest of the system from infections and pathogens alongside being the receptacle for the sperm and the release of menstruation and the exit of the child in childbirth.

The cervix is the muscled gateway that helps fluid flow from the woman's vagina to uterus and vice versa. It helps push the baby through from the uterus to the vagina, it marks the end of the vagina and the start of the uterus and works as a barrier between them. Past the cervix is the uterus, its main job is to house the growing baby when the egg cell is fertilised. When the sperm cells pass through the cervix and vagina they travel through the uterus and across the fallopian tubes to fertilise the egg once the egg is fertilised and the nuclei of the sperm and egg cells combine the now gamete travels down and into the uterus where it embeds in the uterine lining to grow into the foetus. This same uterine lining is what is shed in menstruation every month. This is the endometrium.

The uterus is commonly referred to as looking like an upside-down pear. With two hornlike structures extending out the top, the fallopian tubes, when the foetus is growing the uterus expands filling with amniotic fluid to protect the baby from harm the umbilical is also attached to the placenta and the baby to provide a steady supply of nutrients to the baby.

Finally, there is also the ovaries which release and store the eggs for pregnancy and menstruation. All the eggs a female will ever have are in the ovaries from birth, hence when a woman goes menopausal, they start to run out. The ovaries also release hormones like oestrogen and progesterone which are essential for the menstrual cycle, pregnancy and female sexual characteristics. In conclusion, almost all parts of the female reproductive system revolve around the uterus and fertility making the fertilisation of the egg cell and the growth of the fetus as efficient as possible and making the environment for it as safe and healthy as it can be.

Male Reproductive System

Now we will talk about the male reproductive system, the male reproductive system consists of the testes and urethra and a few more minor parts, the male reproductive system is closely linked to the urinary system which we will touch on soon.

The majority of the male reproductive system is simply the testes, being the factories from which sperm is produced and released from, they are lined and protected by the scrotum, a lining of skin that surrounds them, but remain exceptionally sensitive to pain and damage and can be irreversibly damaged if hit too strongly. Scrotums are very key to the system as they hang out to keep the testes at just the perfect temperature, slightly cooler than the body temp 37 degrees centigrade, allowing them to produce sperm and hormones.

When the sperm is released, it travels down the vas deferens (sperm duct) in a loop around the bladder through the ejaculatory duct where fluid is added to the sperm to make semen by the glands, particularly the prostate gland this nourishes and contain fructose which make the sperm move quicker. The semen then flows through the urethra which runs through the prostate gland and out through the penis.

The penis stiffens and becomes erect during sexual intercourse as it is full of blood vessels, and these blood vessels expand when excited. This makes the penis longer and more solid so it can enter the vagina easier and remain in that position longer.

Hormonal Control

The reproductive systems (both of them) revolve around hormones slightly similar to how the digestive system revolves around enzymes. Without the hormones produced by glands and the ovaries the reproductive systems could not function properly as the testes need the follicle stimulating hormone (FSH) and the luteinizing hormone (LH) to produce and release sperm.

There is also testosterone a hormone that increases muscle mass and fat distribution but also sex drive or libido. As for the female side of thing the as mentioned before oestrogen and progesterone hormones are essential for the female reproductive systems many functions and especially the endometrium.

Alongside this the female reproductive system also uses LH and FSH which like in the male reproductive system are made in the pituitary gland but are used for the development, control and maturation of egg cells.
