The Nervous System

Overview

The nervous system controls the body and allows it to function properly. It is responsible for movement, breathing, thinking and many other processes. Without it, the body would not be able to work.

It is made up of a complex network of neurons and nerves that connect to the brain and spinal cord. The system is divided into two main parts: the central nervous system (CNS), which includes the brain and spinal cord, and the peripheral nervous system (PNS), which includes all the nerves in the body.

You can think of it like a control system. The brain and spinal cord act as the control centre, while the nerves carry messages between the brain and the rest of the body.

Types of Control

The nervous system controls both voluntary and involuntary actions. The somatic nervous system controls voluntary actions such as movement and conscious thought.

The autonomic nervous system controls involuntary actions such as heartbeat, breathing and digestion. These are processes that happen automatically without needing to think about them.

How It Works

The nervous system works in three main steps. First, sensory neurons detect changes in the environment, such as what you see, hear or feel.

Next, the central nervous system processes and stores this information.

Finally, motor neurons send signals to the body to respond, such as moving a muscle or reacting to a stimulus.

These steps are known as sensation, integration and response, and they happen constantly to keep the body functioning.
