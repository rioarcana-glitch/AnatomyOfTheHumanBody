The Circulatory System

Overview

The circulatory system is responsible for transporting important substances around the body. It carries oxygen, nutrients, hormones and blood to where they are needed, while also removing waste products like carbon dioxide.

It is made up of the heart, blood vessels and blood, forming a large network throughout the body.

Structure

The system works through two main pathways: the pulmonary circuit and the systemic circuit. The pulmonary circuit carries blood to the lungs where it picks up oxygen, while the systemic circuit carries oxygenated blood to the rest of the body.

Arteries carry oxygenated blood away from the heart, while veins carry deoxygenated blood back to the heart.

Process

Blood moves around the body in a continuous cycle:

  • Deoxygenated blood is pumped from the heart to the lungs
  • In the lungs, the blood absorbs oxygen
  • Oxygenated blood returns to the heart
  • The heart pumps this blood to the rest of the body
  • Oxygen and nutrients are delivered to cells
  • Waste products are carried back to the lungs to be removed

Importance

The circulatory system is essential for survival. The heart pumps large amounts of blood every day to supply the brain and other vital organs.

Without a constant blood supply, the brain can be damaged within minutes. This shows how important the circulatory system is for keeping the body alive and functioning properly.
