The Immune System

Overview

The immune system is your own personal defence mechanism, it is a vast complex network of cells tissues and organs including the spleen, white blood cells and your lymph nodes. Your immune system works to neutralise pathogens like viruses and bacteria and then to remove the damaged cells from your system to maintain your health.

It responds quickly to small virus and bacteria but can be overwhelmed by more complex, foreign versions, this is why you are given vaccines and inoculation, to train your immune system to fight certain bacteria and illnesses- vaccines inject harmless antigens into the body to make the immune system produce antibodies to fight future illnesses, antigens are typically proteins, polysaccharides, or lipids found on bacteria, viruses, or foreign cells whilst inoculation is where you introduce a smaller or partially destroyed version of a pathogen or microorganism to train the immune system to fight it.

Unfortunately, despite how amazing your immune system is for many cases vaccination and inoculation are crucial and in the rest of this section we’ll be telling you about why this system is so amazing.

Function and Behaviour

Firstly, this system really amazed with how almost human it sounded as the immune system has key humane concepts like memory and adaptive immunity- where the system learns and evolves its responses over time.

In addition your immune system makes mistakes sometimes or in other words malfunctions, this causes allergic reactions, immunodeficiency diseases, or autoimmune disorders, where the body attacks its own cells.

It constantly defends you against pathogens, even when your sleeping white blood cells are racing around your body piecing together your skin and helping your injuries. In fact BILLIONS of microorganisms are defended against by the immune system daily.

Key Components

But what organs, tissues and cells make up this insane system you may ask. Well millions would be the answer but here are some of the key ones:

Lymph Nodes

These are small structures that act almost akin to security checkpoints at airports! They filter lymphatic fluid, trapping and destroying abnormal or cancerous cells and pathogens using white blood cells before returning the fluid to the blood stream. The lymph nodes are located near your groin, armpits and neck areas. These are the inguinal, axillary and cervical lymph nodes respectively. They can often swell when you are ill as fluid and immune cells gather.

White Blood Cells

These are produced in the marrow of your bones and are a key part of your immune system as they are arguably the most essential defence against pathogens.

The Spleen

Is an essential blood filtering organ located in the upper left abdomen that destroys old or damaged blood cells and stores white blood cells.

The Skin

This is a physical barrier that is your biggest organ it stops lots of germs and bacteria from ever entering your body by simply being there as just a hint of dust in your more delicate systems and you can say bye.

Importance

Overall this system spans your whole body constantly working in harmony with the rest of your body to help protect and help your other systems, this improves your bodily efficiency because just imagine having to live with getting a cold every five seconds.

Its an amazing system and is extremely underrated when it comes to its efficiency and importance.
