The Muscular System

Overview

The muscular system is the powerhouse behind the body, it controls movement and executer of the nervous systems commands. Anything you do is powered and completed by the maze of muscles and tendons that lives beneath the skin it is complex and yet so simple. And it fascinates the scientific, sporting and artistic minds and explores the possibilities across a multitude of areas.

Structure

But what makes up this complex system? Well to put it simply, muscles! Obviously, these muscles are siphoned and separated into three separate categories: smooth, skeletal and cardiac.

Smooth muscles enact their purpose involuntarily- they control the contraction and operation of your internal organs, they also contort the movement of vessels and are the only non-striated type of muscles, this is to allow slow sustained contractions and minimal energy usage. (Striated means to have grooves like in skeletal muscles).

Skeletal muscles are connected to, you guessed it: the skeleton, they help move parts of your body by pulling at the bones and forcing them to do things such as locomotion, breathing and joint stability. Besides this, they also maintain posture and produce body heat whilst also acting as a crucial metabolic reservoir- a place to help store energy. They also make up 40% of a humans body mass and are arguably the most important of the muscles in the body.

They’re also highly adaptable and display plasticity, unlike some other muscles, allowing them to adjust strength and size in response to workload, they are helped along with this work primarily by tendons which connect the muscles to bone, this is why when your muscles are in extreme overuse it is very possible to snap one.

As stated in THE NERVOUS SYSTEM (link pls) the somatic nervous system sends signals to make them function as they are voluntary muscles and can only act when you want them to, your autonomic nervous system deals with cardiac and smooth muscles as these need to be constantly working or your body can stop working and you could die.

Cardiac muscles are located in your heart’s walls contracting almost constantly to keep pumping blood throughout your body. In fact, cardiac muscles contract over 100,000 times daily.

Interdependence

Muscles are my favourite example of just how complexly interdependent and how harmonious the body is, as without the muscular system constantly using your energy to pump, contract and move your body, none of the other systems could operate.

The digestive system, for its most fundamental roles uses muscles like chewing, the first stage, which uses skeletal muscles and using the stomach which uses smooth muscles to contract and move it. The circulatory system which would no longer be able to pump blood without muscles and so on…

And yet the digestive system is equally reliant on these same systems for without the digestive system where would the muscular systems energy and fuel come from and without the circulatory system how would those nutrients reach the muscles in the first place?

The body is complex thing and yet the ways it works is not, it is like most things in nature harmonious and perfect and truly marvellous to ponder at.
