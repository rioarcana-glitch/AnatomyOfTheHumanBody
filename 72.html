Respiratory System

Overview

The respiratory system is a relatively condensed system compared to some of the other system’s metres of nerves, veins organs and cells spread throughout the body, despite this it remains one of the most impactful, rivalling the cardiovascular system in its essentialism.

The system revolves around the lungs and the diaphragm powerful organs located in the centre of your chest and these alongside network of tissues airways and blood vessels surrounding them work to pump oxygen into the body and to remove waste like carbon dioxide and lactic acid from your system.

Breathing Process

The respiratory system begins its job when you breathe in, you do this roughly 20,000 to 25,000 times a day so its a hard working and efficient system.

When you breathe in, your diaphragm contracts and moves downward this increases the space you have in your chest-your chest cavity- your muscles between your ribs also help expand the space allowing your lungs to move into it.

This moves your rib cage up and outwards and is why you sometimes feel like your whole upper body goes up when you inhale, its natural!

Air Movement

Anyway, as your lungs expand air is sucked in through your nose and mouth down your trachea- windpipe- then into your lungs where it travels down one of your two bronchi and then through one of your bronchioles.

These are your bronchial tubes that run throughout your lungs and are commonly known as your airways, at the end of your bronchioles are millions of tiny air sacs called alveoli these sacs have extremely thin walls and on the other side of these walls are blood vessels, veins and capillaries.

Gas Exchange

The oxygen you inhale then passes through these thin walls on to the blood vessels beyond and gas exchange occurs- where carbon dioxide and oxygen switch places- and the oxygen is picked up by haemoglobin in the blood and transported around the body to create energy.

Breathing Out

As this process occurs your diaphragm relaxes and your chest cavity lessens moving your ribcage down and in and most importantly forcing all the carbon dioxide and other gases that are in your lungs back the way they came and out through your mouth.

Because remember that 78% of our air is nitrogen and a small 21% is oxygen and 1% is carbon dioxide and other gases so that alongside all the carbon dioxide that is produced as a waste product of making energy you have a lot to get rid of.

Efficiency and Importance

Now you might be thinking that this sounds like a pretty lengthy process if so then you would be completely wrong, no your amazing respiratory system does all of this in just 3 to 5 seconds!

It works in harmony with the muscular and cardiovascular systems to efficiently pump an essential gas into the body and to remove waste products
