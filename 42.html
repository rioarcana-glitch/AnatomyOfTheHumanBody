The Circulatory System

Overview

The next system we will talk about is the circulatory system. It is a vast and complex maze full of winding veins pumping arteries and coarse capillaries in a network of over 60000 miles long and with over a billion separate blood vessels. Like in the nervous system there is a centre, a command point even, to this vast system, the heart.

The circulatory system is like a delivery system, delivering and supplying all the necessary nutrients, blood, oxygen, hormones and so much more whilst also removing the chemical waste like lactic acid and carbon dioxide.

Structure

The circulatory system works through two major channels, the pulmonary and systemic the pulmonary circuit supplies blood to the lungs where the blood absorbs oxygen and then joins the systemic circuit.

The circulatory system is systematic and extremely effective as whilst oxygenated blood goes to the rest of the body through arteries the deoxygenated blood travels back to the heart through veins. This is often represented through the colours of blue and red in diagrams.

Process

Specifically, this is how blood is driven round this cycle:

  • Deoxygenated blood is pumped vigorously through your heart’s right ventricle. Through the ventricle the blood then travels, heading towards your lungs along the way it then passes through the pulmonary trunk: the main pulmonary artery.
  • Then the blood picks up oxygen in the lungs and is then oxygenated
  • Pulmonary veins carry oxygenated blood to your heart’s left atrium
  • The left atrium sends the oxygenated blood to your left ventricle which pumps blood through your main arteries to the rest of the body
  • Blood then transfers oxygen nutrients and hormones to the rest of the body
  • It is then transferred back to the lungs and carbon dioxide is transferred into the lungs to be exhaled

Importance

Your heart circulates roughly 2000 gallons of blood through your body every day, specifically your blood is pumped through to your brain and heart as these are your most essential organs.

In fact, just seconds without blood flow to your brain can lead to the loss of consciousness and in four minutes you can gain permanent damage. This shows the importance of the circulatory system as it is the fuel and sustenance your body relies on to function and perform to its maximum ability.
