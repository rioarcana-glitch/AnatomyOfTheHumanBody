Skeletal System

Overview

The skeletal system is the key to the most mundane things that you probably dont even think about before doing them. These include posture, healing, storing of essential nutrients, movement and even just retaining your humanoid form.

You see all of the body is built around the bones, like branches off a tree, the muscles for example are directly attached to your bones and the nerves all stem from the spine. Without the framework of your bones you word be a pile of jelly on the floor.

The skeletal system consists of 206 bones in the average human alongside a dense network of ligaments, cartilage and joints. Apart from the staple roles that connotate with this system it also performs a variety of other essential functions including storing minerals like calcium that are essential to your growth and looks and also providing blood cells which is arguably the important of its roles.

Structure and Development

This system is incredibly complex with as said before 206 separate bones in an adult and a whopping over 300 in a child. These bones are held together using fibrous connective tissue called ligaments which are insanely stretchy and tough as they withstand the almost constant blunting force of your moving bones against them.

Now you might be wondering why it is that children have more bones than adults, surely it would be just the other way round or equal amounts on both sides? Well no, children have over 300 bones because at birth their bones are made completely from calcium, a flexible but tough connective tissue that helps them curl up in the womb, but stretchable and easily bendable bones are not useful in the outside world and so this cartilage is slowly replaced by the hard bones we know in older years but during this process lots of your bones fuse together, knitting together to form solid more compound structures.

This is why babies should be treated so carefully because one drop and all those tiny brittle bones can break and not be replaced or fuse together right!

Anyway, onto a happier topic, bone growth and reshape doesn’t actually stop there your bones even in adulthood are constantly reshaping and remodelling. Fact time, every ten years your skeleton completely replaces itself, crazy! Anyway, this whole process, bone development, is formally known as ossification and starts at eight weeks of embryonic development.

Key Bones

206 bones is still a lot so lets narrow it down to your ten most interesting ones:

The Spine

The spine runs up the middle of your back from the base of the skull to the tail bone and is also part of your nervous system, thousands of nerves stream off it carrying direct orders straight from the brain itself to the rest of your body the spine helps you stand up straight and is made up of 33 vertebrae its most key role though is too protect your spinal cord which takes the messages from the brain directly.

The Skull

The skull is a dense bone structure which acts as an easel for the painting of your facial features, supporting upholding and framing them. Everyone's skull is different but they all protect the brain as their main role.

The Ribcage

The ribcage is a series of ribs that act as major protectors to all the vital organs in your chest protecting and supporting them, it is also directly connected to the spine.

The Funny Bone

The funny bone, well its not actually a bone and is instead the ulnar nerve connected to your humorous, funny, the bone in your upper arm.

The Femur

The femur is the longest bone in your body stretching over 18 inches in the average adult human, it is also the strongest bone in your body as it can support up to 30 times the weight of your body. It's the only bone in your thigh.

The Stapes

The stapes or stirrup is the smallest bone in your body it is used in hearing alongside three other bones called the ossicles in your ear using vibrations and is less than 3 millimetres long, less than a grain of rice.

The Hyoid Bone

The hyoid bone, apart from having a cool name this bone is the only bone not connected to any other and is used to support the tongue in speaking.

The Clavicle

The clavicle is a shoulder bone also known as the beauty bone, it is the only long bone to lie horizontally.

The Coccyx

The coccyx is located near the base of the spine and shows the remains of a tail that past ancestors used to have this is why if you feel there, there is a lump.

The Intermediate Cuneiform

And finally, the intermediate cuneiform the bone with the longest name and a wedge-shaped tarsal in the foot.
