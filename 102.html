Exocrine and Endocrine Systems

Overview and Differences

I decided to combine these two systems into one as they are similar in many ways and contain similar organs and structures.

I’ll start with outlining the differences between these two systems, the exocrine system is a large network of glands that secrete substances into ducts which deliver these substances into the surface of tissues.

The endocrine on the other hand is a large network of glands that produce hormones that are transferred directly to the blood stream, which delivers these hormones into tissues.

Exocrine System

The exocrine system specifically supplies substances to mostly these tissues: breasts, mouth, skin, hair and small intestine.

To do this the exocrine system uses a variety of glands that then produce the substance needed for that tissue.

Mammary Glands

The mammary glands are designed to produce breast milk in breast feeding so that the baby can be nourished and its immune system bolstered.

Salivary and Mucinous Glands

A type of serous glands known as the salivatory glands in the mouth produce saliva and are designed to help kickstart the digestive process by breaking down carbohydrates and to help protect and lubricate the throat and mouth, another gland in the mouth is the mucinous gland which as the name suggests produces mucus a substance that lubricates the food you eat to make the digestive process easier.

Sweat Glands

Another type of serous gland is the sweat gland which, as the name suggests, helps you sweat, there are two types of these, the eccrine are everywhere and produce the sweat itself whilst the apocrine produce a substance that stops the sweat from evaporating for longer therefore keeping your skin cooler for longer.

Sebaceous and Mucosal Glands

Finally, there are also the sebaceous glands which produce oil to protect your hair and skin and mucosal glands which protect the small intestine from harm from the stomach’s acids.

Endocrine System

The endocrine system produces hormones; hormones are like messengers they carry commands to cells and organs monitoring and controlling things like metabolism and homeostasis.

The endocrine system contains many of the organs in your body like the testes, ovaries, kidneys, liver and heart as they all produce their own specific types of hormones, but for the sake of simplicity I will only include some of the major glands.

Pituitary Gland

The pituitary gland is the most important of the glands in this system as it is the boss, it produces the hormones that make the other glands do what they need to do.

Pineal Gland

Another important gland is the pineal gland which, like pituitary gland, is in the brain it produces melatonin.

Other Glands

Alongside these there are also many others like the thyroid which help control metabolism and the adrenal which helps control stress response and blood pressure.
