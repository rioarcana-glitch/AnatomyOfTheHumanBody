The Nervous System

The nervous system is the remote control to the body controlling essential things like movement, breathing thought and so much more, without the nervous system none of the other systems could function at the efficiency that they do. But what is the nervous system you may ask? Well, the nervous system a complex labyrinth of neurons and nerves all feeding back to the spinal cord and the big boss itself the brain.

The nervous system is divided majorly into two parts: the CNS (the central nervous system) and the PNS (the peripheral nervous system). The CNS consists of the brain and spinal cord and the PNS, all of the nerves that make up the nervous system.

I personally like to think of it as a slightly outdated remote-controlled machine where the machine is wired to the remote. The body is like the machine, without the remote control it is lifeless and without purpose or ability to do anything, the CNS is like the remote itself, able to make the body/machine move and perform its purpose and finally the PNS is like the wires which allow the ideas thoughts and commands from the remote/CNS to reach the machine/body.

The nervous system makes a whole range of things happen inside the body, the list is endless from knowing you need to excrete yourself to keeping your heart beating. But hang on, you don’t think about keeping your heart beating at least not actively so how does the nervous system make it beat constantly? This is where the nervous systems two main parts come into play: the somatic part of the nervous system, which controls voluntary movements and relays sensory information (the stuff you think about) and the automatic part of the nervous system which controls the involuntary functions (things you don't even realise you're doing like digestion).

Now the basics have been explained, we will explain in depth how exactly the nervous system operates.

As a rough breakdown of what happens: the sensory neurons pick up information about what you are seeing hearing sensing and feeling from the environment, the CNS then gathers processes and stores this information and then sends commands via the motor neurons to all the other organs and systems in your body they then perform these actions.

These three steps are known as sensation integration and response. Almost all activity in the nervous system follows these steps. The sensory receptors take external information to the brain and spinal cord whilst motor pathways are for movement commanded by the brain.
