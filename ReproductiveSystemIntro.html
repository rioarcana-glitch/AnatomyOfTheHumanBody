The Male Reproductive System

The male reproductive system consists primarily of the testes, urethra, and associated structures, and is closely linked to the urinary system. The testes act as the primary site of sperm production and are protected by the scrotum, a sac of skin that maintains them at a temperature slightly below normal body temperature, which is necessary for effective sperm production.

Once produced, sperm travels through the vas deferens, looping around the bladder and entering the ejaculatory duct. During this process, fluids are added by glands such as the prostate gland to form semen. These fluids provide nutrients, including fructose, which increases sperm mobility. The semen then passes through the urethra and exits the body through the penis.

The penis contains a large network of blood vessels, which expand during sexual arousal, causing it to become erect. This allows for the effective transfer of sperm into the female reproductive system during intercourse.

Hormonal Control

The reproductive system is heavily regulated by hormones, much like the digestive system relies on enzymes. In males, hormones such as follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH) and luteinising hormone (LH) are essential for the production and release of sperm. Testosterone also plays a key role, influencing muscle development, fat distribution, and libido.

In females, hormones such as oestrogen and progesterone are crucial for regulating the menstrual cycle, maintaining the endometrium, and supporting pregnancy. Similar to males, FSH and LH are produced by the pituitary gland and are responsible for controlling the development and release of egg cells. These hormonal interactions ensure that both reproductive systems function correctly and in coordination.
