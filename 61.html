The Immune System

Overview

The immune system protects the body from harmful pathogens such as viruses and bacteria. It is made up of a network of cells, tissues and organs that work together to keep the body healthy.

It removes harmful substances and damaged cells while helping the body fight infections.

How It Works

The immune system responds quickly to threats, but sometimes it needs help. Vaccines and inoculations train the immune system to recognise and fight specific pathogens.

Vaccines introduce harmless antigens into the body, allowing it to produce antibodies for future protection. This helps the body respond faster if the real pathogen appears.

Function and Behaviour

The immune system has a form of memory, meaning it can remember and respond more effectively to pathogens it has encountered before.

However, it can sometimes make mistakes, leading to allergies, autoimmune diseases or weakened immune responses.

It constantly works to protect the body, defending against billions of microorganisms every day.

Main Components

The immune system includes several important parts:

  • Lymph nodes: Filter harmful substances and contain immune cells
  • White blood cells: Fight infections and destroy pathogens
  • Spleen: Filters blood and removes damaged cells
  • Skin: Acts as a protective barrier against pathogens

Importance

The immune system is essential for survival. Without it, the body would constantly be vulnerable to illness.

It works with other systems to maintain health and protect the body from harm.
