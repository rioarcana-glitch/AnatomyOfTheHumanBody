The Digestive System

The digestive system is a compilation of organs that work together to break down and absorb the necessary nutrients to survive, grow and repair. The digestive system is a combination of vital organs that break down and absorb nutrients and food through mechanical or chemical digestion. The digestive system mainly consists of the gastrointestinal tract and several accessory organs. The main components include: Mouth, Oesophagus, Stomach, Small intestine, Large Intestine. And there are other parts like the Liver and Pancreas, which are vital to the system. The digestive system is a very complex system of parts that all operate to degrade food and absorb nutrients to do this the system uses enzymes which are biological catalysts (help speed up reactions). But enzymes cannot exist without a certain amount of acidity or alkali and so the digestive system adapts and changes the ph levels of different parts of the gastrointestinal tract so that every enzyme can work in perfect harmony to break down and digest food.

The Mouth

The Mouth is the entry point for the digestive process where the food begins to be broken down by saliva. The salivary glands are where the saliva is made. Your teeth also grind and tear food into a more digestible size. You also use the tongue to help digest the food and make it easier to digest.

The Oesophagus / Gullet and Stomach

The oesophagus is the muscular tube that transports food from the mouth to the stomach through peristalsis (wave-like muscular contractions). The tongue pushes the food into the oesophagus. The oesophagus is an amazing organ due to as stated before its ph levels. Across the span of the oesophagus many enzymes are used to help speed up the digestion of food for example lipase carbohydrase and protease. But these enzymes can only exist in certain environments, so like the amazing host it is, the body provides the oesophagus is split into multiple parts all with different environments (temps and acidity levels) to suit the needs of the inhabitant enzyme.

The food moves down into the stomach where the food is covered in hydrochloric acid. This combined with enzymes further breaks down food. This is an incredibly strong acid that can even dissolve teeth; its ph is around 1. The stomach is lined with mucus so that it doesn’t dissolve itself and the rest of the body. It works as the most major part in dissolvement of the food.

The Liver

The liver has many vital functions in the human body some of these are: makes cholesterol that your body uses in different ways, helps produce certain hormones, protects your cells and is key in the production of bile, it makes proteins like clotting factors that manage bleeding and albumin, which manages fluid pressure in your bloodstream. It helps keep your blood glucose levels steady by storing glycogen (glucose) and releasing it into your bloodstream to keep your blood sugar levels on an even amount, breaks down toxins and germs so they safely leave your body in your pee and poop. Works by breaking down fats in your blood to produce energy, and if there are too many, they may get stored as extra fat.

The Small Intestine

The small intestine compromises of three main parts, the duodenum, jejunum and the ileum. The small intestine is where most nutrients are absorbed into the bloodstream. Digestive enzymes from the pancreas and bile from the liver aid in this process. By breaking down the strong molecular structures of the food until it is small enough to be captured by villi (small hairlike structures that line both intestines). For examples carbohydrates in the body are broken down from a long string of simple sugar nutrients into separate sugar nutrients which are just small enough for the villi to catch. These simple sugars are then small enough to be absorbed into the blood stream.

The Large Intestine

The large intestine wraps around the small intestine it has a similar job but instead of nutrients (which are now all gone) it transfers water and electrolytes into the blood stream it also helps create vitamins B and K. Leaving just waste products which are then transferred into the rectum. The appendix is known for being useless and removable but just like every other part of the digestive system it is vital to the smooth running along of this complex job. Whilst it can be removed from the body, there is a reason it’s in there. The appendix helps you store useful bacteria that help you dissolve and break down the food you eat so it can’t be flushed out by diarrhoea or antibiotics.

The Gallbladder and Spleen

The gall bladder and spleen are two other examples of small less well-known organs. The gallbladder can be removed just like the appendix, but it has arguably a more important job. The gallbladder helps to break down lipids (fats) into smaller parts in the small intestine, duodenum by concentrating and storing bile, a strong alkaline fluid made in the liver. The spleen on the other hand is not actually part of the digestive system, contrast to popular belief but as we are not including the smaller systems like the lymphatic system, which the spleen is located in, we thought we might as well include it. The spleen acts as the nurse in the blood, filtering out the old or damaged red blood cells and sending out antibodies to fight off germs and infections in the body.

The Rectum and Anus

The rectum and anus are our last stops in the digestive system as they help get what’s left and the stuff we don’t need out of our body. The rectum is the very end of the gastrointestinal tract and the collector of all you left over faeces, poop and excess water. It holds these until the body is ready to release or has to release, due to too many faeces build up then it releases your waste down the anal canal and out your anus into wherever you decided to defecate. And so there you have it the digestive system working in complete and utter harmony to release all the nutrients of food into your bloodstream and leave all that you don’t need left in just 14–58 hours!
