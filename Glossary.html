  • Digestive System: A group of organs that break down food and absorb nutrients.
  • Gastrointestinal Tract: The pathway food travels through the body.
  • Mechanical Digestion: Physical breakdown of food.
  • Chemical Digestion: Breakdown using enzymes and acids.
  • Enzyme: A biological catalyst that speeds up reactions.
  • Biological Catalyst: A substance that speeds reactions without being used up.
  • pH: A scale measuring acidity or alkalinity.
  • Salivary Glands: Produce saliva in the mouth.
  • Oesophagus: Tube that carries food to the stomach.
  • Peristalsis: Wave-like contractions that move food.
  • Lipase: Breaks down fats.
  • Carbohydrase: Breaks down carbohydrates.
  • Protease: Breaks down proteins.
  • Hydrochloric Acid: Strong stomach acid for digestion.
  • Mucus: Protects the stomach lining.
  • Liver: Produces bile and processes nutrients.
  • Pancreas: Produces digestive enzymes.
  • Small Intestine: Absorbs nutrients.
  • Duodenum: First part of the small intestine.
  • Jejunum: Middle part of the small intestine.
  • Ileum: Final part of the small intestine.
  • Villi: Absorb nutrients into the bloodstream.
  • Large Intestine: Absorbs water and forms waste.
  • Electrolytes: Minerals that maintain balance.
  • Appendix: Stores useful bacteria.
  • Gallbladder: Stores bile.
  • Bile: Helps break down fats.
  • Lipids: Fats used for energy.
  • Rectum: Stores waste.
  • Anus: Where waste exits the body.
  • Faeces: Solid waste.

Reproductive System

  • Reproductive System: Organs involved in reproduction.
  • Fertilisation: When sperm meets egg.
  • Sperm Cell: Male reproductive cell.
  • Egg Cell: Female reproductive cell.
  • Gamete: A reproductive cell.
  • Fetus: A developing baby.
  • Menstruation: Monthly shedding of the uterine lining.
  • Vagina: Connects uterus to outside.
  • Cervix: Opening between uterus and vagina.
  • Uterus: Where a baby develops.
  • Endometrium: Uterine lining.
  • Fallopian Tube: Where fertilisation occurs.
  • Ovaries: Produce eggs and hormones.
  • Oestrogen: Female hormone.
  • Progesterone: Supports pregnancy.
  • Menopause: End of menstrual cycle.
  • Amniotic Fluid: Protects the baby.
  • Placenta: Supplies nutrients to baby.
  • Umbilical Cord: Connects baby to placenta.
  • Testes: Produce sperm.
  • Scrotum: Holds testes.
  • Sperm: Male reproductive cells.
  • Vas Deferens: Carries sperm.
  • Ejaculatory Duct: Moves semen.
  • Semen: Fluid containing sperm.
  • Prostate Gland: Adds fluid to semen.
  • Urethra: Carries urine and semen.
  • Penis: Male reproductive organ.
  • Testosterone: Male hormone.
  • Libido: Sex drive.
  • Hormone: Chemical messenger.
  • FSH (Follicle Stimulating Hormone): Controls egg/sperm production.
  • LH (Luteinizing Hormone): Triggers ovulation and sperm release.
  • Pituitary Gland: Controls hormone release.

Nervous System

  • Nervous System: Controls body functions.
  • Neuron: Nerve cell.
  • Central Nervous System (CNS): Brain and spinal cord.
  • Peripheral Nervous System (PNS): All other nerves.
  • Spinal Cord: Connects brain to body.
  • Somatic Nervous System: Controls voluntary actions.
  • Autonomic Nervous System: Controls automatic functions.
  • Sensory Neurons: Carry signals to brain.
  • Motor Neurons: Carry commands from brain.
  • Sensory Receptors: Detect changes.
  • Stimulus: A change that triggers a response.
  • Response: Reaction to a stimulus.
  • Integration: Processing information.

Circulatory System

  • Circulatory System: Transports blood and nutrients.
  • Arteries: Carry blood away from heart.
  • Veins: Carry blood to heart.
  • Capillaries: Tiny exchange vessels.
  • Heart: Pumps blood.
  • Pulmonary Circuit: Blood flow to lungs.
  • Systemic Circuit: Blood flow to body.
  • Oxygenated Blood: Blood with oxygen.
  • Deoxygenated Blood: Blood without oxygen.
  • Atrium: Upper heart chamber.
  • Ventricle: Lower heart chamber.
  • Pulmonary Artery: Carries blood to lungs.
  • Pulmonary Vein: Carries blood to heart.
  • Carbon Dioxide: Waste gas removed from body.
