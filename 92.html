Urinary System

Overview

The urinary system as the name suggests helps people urinate but it also does so much more, this one really surprised me as I researched it as before I just assumed all it did was help you excrete but I was very wrong.

This system is fundamental for the filtration of your blood, controlling of your blood pressure and the removal and storage of liquid waste.

Now here’s a question you’ve probably never asked yourself before, how does your wee form? It's actually very interesting: you see urine is mainly made of waste products from blood like excess water and other toxins or waste.

And these are filtered straight from your blood to retain something called homeostasis.

Now homeostasis is a really cool concept your body uses its the act of maintaining a stable balanced internal environment for your organs a bit like how in your oesophagus it maintains lots of different Ph levels.

Homeostasis is essential for things like blood and glucose which have a narrow survival range in temperatures, homeostasis controls major things like hydration, temperature and Ph- acidity and alkaline levels.

Process

The process the urinary system follows is like so:

  1. Blood flows into your kidneys through lots of tiny capillaries, there the kidneys separate toxins from nutrients, letting nutrients, proteins, minerals and vitamins back into the bloodstream and pulling toxins, salts and excess water down into your ureters.
  2. The ureters carry these toxins, which mixed with the water makes pee, down to the bladder where they are stored
  3. The bladder can hold roughly 0.5 litres of urine and when it gets full the muscles around it relax and it contracts forcing the urine out through the urethra

Fun Stats

Fun stats: your kidneys filter roughly 114 to 142 litres of blood a day and take roughly 1-2 litres of pee from this amount.

The urinary system is actually vital to your survival so treat it with care.

Pee Colours

This is quite a fun topic to finish up on as I’ll talk about why your pee comes out different colours.

This is mostly because of your levels of hydration, fewer water means higher concentration of toxins and other nasty stuff, but its also because of a pigment called urochrome which comes from the breakdown of old red blood cells, obviously a higher ratio of urochrome to water leads to more concentration of the pigment, deepening the colour.

But your pee can also change colour from a variety of medical conditions, vitamin intake and vegetables, my personal favourite is beetroot which turns your pee pink!
