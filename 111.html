The Integumentary System

Overview

The integumentary system is the largest organ system in the body. It includes the skin, hair, nails and related glands.

Its main role is to protect the body from the outside environment while helping maintain homeostasis, including temperature and water balance.

Main Functions

The integumentary system performs many important jobs, including protection, sensation, temperature regulation and excretion.

It works closely with other body systems, especially the immune system, to help keep the body healthy.

The Skin

Epidermis

The epidermis is the outer layer of the skin. It protects the body from water loss and physical damage.

Dermis

The dermis contains sweat glands, oil glands and hair follicles.

Hypodermis

The hypodermis is the deepest layer and contains fat that helps insulate the body.

Nails

Nails protect the ends of the fingers and toes.

Important parts include the nail bed, nail plate, cuticle, matrix and lunula.

Hair

Hair grows across much of the body, including the head, arms, legs, armpits and groin.

Its main function is insulation, helping the body retain heat and maintain a stable temperature.

Importance

The integumentary system protects the body, helps regulate temperature and allows us to feel sensations such as heat, cold and touch.

Without it, the body would struggle to maintain balance and protect itself from the environment.
