The Muscular System

Overview

The muscular system allows the body to move and carry out actions. It works closely with the nervous system, which sends signals to control muscle movement.

Muscles and tendons work together to power everything the body does, from simple movements to complex actions.

Types of Muscles

There are three main types of muscles: smooth, skeletal and cardiac.

Smooth muscles work automatically and control internal organs and blood vessels. They are designed for slow, steady movement.

Skeletal muscles are attached to bones and control movement such as walking, breathing and maintaining posture. They also produce heat and store energy.

Cardiac muscle is found in the heart and works continuously to pump blood around the body.

Function

Skeletal muscles are controlled by the somatic nervous system, meaning they move when you choose to move them. Smooth and cardiac muscles are controlled automatically by the autonomic nervous system.

Muscles are adaptable and can change size and strength depending on how much they are used. Tendons connect muscles to bones and help transfer force.

Importance

The muscular system is essential for movement and survival. It works with other systems such as the digestive and circulatory systems to keep the body functioning.

Without muscles, the body would not be able to move, pump blood or process food, showing how important this system is.
