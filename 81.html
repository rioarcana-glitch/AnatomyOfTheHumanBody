Skeletal System

Overview

The skeletal system gives the body structure, support and shape. It allows movement, protects organs and stores important minerals.

It is made up of 206 bones, along with ligaments, cartilage and joints.

Functions

The skeletal system helps with:

  • Movement (working with muscles)
  • Protection of organs
  • Storing minerals like calcium
  • Producing blood cells

Growth and Development

Babies are born with over 300 bones. As they grow, some bones fuse together, leaving 206 bones in adulthood.

Bones are constantly changing and repairing themselves throughout life. This process is called ossification.

Important Bones

  • Spine: Supports the body and protects the spinal cord
  • Skull: Protects the brain
  • Ribcage: Protects organs like the heart and lungs
  • Femur: The longest and strongest bone
  • Stapes: The smallest bone, used for hearing
  • Clavicle: A horizontal shoulder bone
  • Coccyx: The tailbone

Importance

Without the skeletal system, the body would not be able to stand, move or protect its vital organs.

It works closely with muscles and other systems to keep the body functioning properly.
