The Integumentary System

Overview

Funnily enough, despite being one of the least well-known organ systems in the body the integumentary system is the largest containing massive organs like the skin and hair alongside your nails and associated organs.

The integumentary system is key to homeostasis, the regulating of internal Ph and temperature to maximise organ efficiency, this is because it is the main barrier to the internal and the external it uses concepts like UV protection perspiration and thermoregulation.

Functions

The integumentary system has many functions like sensation and excretion as just an example making it one of the most crucial of all the systems.

It is the scales that helps hold your body in balance and is the ultimate projection of harmony in the human anatomy as it works with almost all the bodily systems to maintain balance and protect your body.

For example, the integumentary system works alongside the immune system to protect your body from bacteria with its massive physical barrier, the skin.

The Skin

This is the main organ of this system, and it is comprised of three separate layers: the epidermis (outer layer), the dermis (inner layer) and the hypodermis (the deepest layer made mostly of fat).

Epidermis

The epidermis is the front lines with its main jobs being water protection and protection against other types of physical harm, it is what you see when you look at your skin and gives it its colour.

Dermis

The dermis is the thickest layer, containing sweat and oil glands and hair follicles.

Hypodermis

Finally, the hypodermis is the final layer, and it helps insulate your body using fat.

Nails

Your nails are another part of this system with their main function being to protect the tops of your fingers and toes.

They are made up of mostly five separate parts these being the nail bed (the skin beneath your nail), the nail plate (the hard bit you can see), the cuticle (the thin skin at the base of your nail plate), the matrix (the root of your nail that makes it grow, you can’t see this one) and the lunula (the white semicircle at the base of your nail).

Hair

The last key part of the integumentary system is the hair, now when I say this you probably immediately think about the longer strands on your head, but the hair is everywhere covering most key areas of the skin: pubic and armpit hair are just two of the many places where your hair grows, the forearms and legs are also common places.

Across your body these tiny hairs form and grow, but what are they for? Insulation is the main function of hair.

This is evident as your hair grows heavily in key places where insulation and warmth are essential like the armpits and groin where heat is lost.

Importance

As said before, your integumentary system is one of the most evident examples of harmony in the body, it works with almost all your bodily systems enhancing and helping them complete their roles, without it your body would be left in shambles.

No protection from anything, no insulation, no homeostasis and no sensory reception or feeling like hot or cold!
