Respiratory System

Overview

The respiratory system allows the body to take in oxygen and remove carbon dioxide. It mainly involves the lungs and diaphragm.

It works with other systems to provide oxygen for energy and remove waste gases.

Breathing In

When you breathe in, your diaphragm moves down and your chest expands. This allows your lungs to fill with air.

Air enters through the nose or mouth, travels down the trachea, and into the lungs.

Inside the Lungs

Inside the lungs, air moves through bronchi and bronchioles to tiny air sacs called alveoli.

These sacs are surrounded by blood vessels where gas exchange takes place.

Gas Exchange

Oxygen passes into the blood and is carried around the body to produce energy.

Carbon dioxide moves from the blood into the lungs to be removed.

Breathing Out

When you breathe out, your diaphragm relaxes and your chest moves inward, pushing air out of the lungs.

Importance

The respiratory system works very quickly, completing this process in just a few seconds.

It is essential for life and works closely with the circulatory and muscular systems.
